# Granyyte voice: how to be funny without being a clown

Read with the Writing rules in CLAUDE.md, which always win. This file adds the humour layer and the local positioning. It's a draft to tune once Chand pastes 5-10 of his LinkedIn/Facebook posts.

## The one-line version
A senior engineer who has seen every way software projects go wrong, finds most of it funny, and tells you the truth even when it loses us the job.

## Dial by page type
| Page | Humour level | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Blog posts | **High.** A joke or wry aside every few paragraphs, a funny H2 now and then | People read posts. Readers who laugh stay, and dwell time is a ranking signal |
| Sialkot / Pakistan landing pages | **Medium.** One sharp line per section at most, mostly in intros and FAQ answers | Buyers skim these. Wit makes us memorable; the answer still comes first |
| Service pages, home, international landing pages | **Low.** Dry, understated, the occasional bracketed aside | Western buyers are checking whether we're serious |
| Legal, contact form, errors | None | |

## What our humour is
- **Self-aware about the industry.** The $99 template. The "expert" who learned WordPress last Ramadan. The freelancer who vanishes after milestone two. The WhatsApp group that is secretly the company's ERP. The Excel sheet named `FINAL_final_v3_REAL.xlsx`.
- **Dry and specific.** "The site loads in 9 seconds, which is roughly how long a buyer in Hamburg waits before closing the tab." Specific beats zany.
- **Bracketed asides.** "(We checked. It was a nulled theme.)"
- **Honest about ourselves.** "We're not the cheapest. We're also not the people you call again in six months to rebuild it."
- **Short punchline after a long sentence.** Long setup, explains the thing properly, builds a picture. Then the punch. Done.

## What our humour is not
- Never laughing at the client or their problem. We laugh at the *practice* that caused it.
- Never naming a competitor, a person, or a real company we didn't build for.
- No memes, no "lol", no emoji, no exclamation-mark enthusiasm.
- A joke never replaces the answer. Answer the question in the first sentence, joke in the second if at all.
- No invented anecdotes dressed as real ("A client once told us..."). Only stories from `stories.md`. A hypothetical must be clearly hypothetical ("Picture a buyer in Germany...").

## The local positioning (Chand, 2026-11-13). This is the spine of Sialkot and Pakistan copy
> Plenty of developers here will build you a website for 10,000 or 20,000 rupees. What you get is a prebuilt WordPress theme with your logo swapped in, from someone who calls themselves an expert and has never shipped anything for a client abroad. No security hardening, no real SEO, code nobody can maintain, nothing that scales. If you need a few pages and none of that matters to you, honestly, hire them. If you want an engineer to build your website, your software or your app, that's us.

How to use it:
- The PKR figures are **market prices, not ours**, which CLAUDE.md allows. We never state our own number, not even "we start at".
- Always include the honest out ("if you just need a few pages, go for it"). It's the line that makes the rest believable.
- Concrete, generic-and-true details only: the WordPress login sitting at `/wp-admin` with `admin` as the username, 30 plugins and none updated, a theme bought once and used for 40 clients, a contact form that emails nobody, a 6 MB hero image on a 4G connection, no sitemap, every page titled "Home".
- Back it with proof nobody local can fake: apps live on both stores for Turkey, Poland, the UAE and the US, 15,000+ Bilge AI users, the UK surveying firm's operations platform, the FlutterFlow certification.
- Agency "we" voice: "that's us", never "I'm here".
- Lighter version on Pakistan pages (same template problem nationwide). International pages don't get it.

## Rhythm checklist (run on every draft)
- [ ] First sentence answers or hooks. No "In today's..." warm-up.
- [ ] Sentence length varies. At least one under 6 words per section.
- [ ] Contractions everywhere.
- [ ] At least one specific name, number or place per section (from `stories.md` / `projects.ts`).
- [ ] One honest tradeoff or "don't hire us if..." somewhere.
- [ ] No em/en dashes, no banned words, no rule-of-three pile-ups.
- [ ] Read it aloud. If it sounds like a brochure, cut the adjective and add a fact.
