Export order tracking software is something most Sialkot exporters already have. It's called WhatsApp. Plus an Excel file named `ORDERS_FINAL_final_v3_REAL.xlsx`, plus one person, usually the merchandiser, who actually knows where everything is.

It works. Until that person goes on leave.

Here's the short answer. Proper export order tracking software puts every order in one place, from the buyer's first inquiry through sampling, production, QC and shipment, so anyone in the company can see where an order stands without calling the floor. For a lot of Sialkot manufacturers, that's the single most useful piece of software they don't have yet.

## How orders are tracked today (be honest)

Picture a typical exporter in Sialkot. Sports goods, gloves, apparel, surgical instruments, it doesn't really matter. The flow looks like this:

- An inquiry arrives by email.
- The details get forwarded to a WhatsApp group.
- Someone makes a sample, takes a photo and posts it in a different WhatsApp group.
- The buyer approves on email. Someone updates the Excel sheet. Maybe.
- Production updates arrive as voice notes.
- QC happens, and the photos live on one inspector's phone.
- The shipment date is in someone's head.

Then the buyer emails: "Any update on PO 4471?" And four people spend forty minutes finding out.

Nobody is doing anything wrong. The tools just weren't made for this. WhatsApp is a chat app pretending to be an operations system, and it's very good at pretending right up until it isn't.

## What export order tracking software should cover

One record per order, moving through clear stages:

1. **Inquiry.** Buyer, product, quantities, target price, deadline. Every inquiry logged, even the ones that don't convert. They tell you something too.
2. **Sampling.** Sample sent, date, courier, buyer comments, approved or revised. Revision three shouldn't be a mystery.
3. **Order.** PO number, sizes, colours, quantities, delivery date, terms.
4. **Materials.** What's needed, what's ordered, what's arrived. This is where it connects to stock, which we covered in [inventory software for a Sialkot godown](/blog/inventory-management-system-sialkot).
5. **Production.** Stages that match your floor: cutting, stitching, finishing, packing, whatever yours are. Updated from a phone, by the people doing the work.
6. **QC.** Inspection results with photos attached to the order, not to someone's gallery. Pass, fail, rework.
7. **Shipment.** Packing list, carton count, shipping date, terms, documents.

Plus a dashboard that answers the only question management really asks: what's late, and why?

## The features that actually save time

- **Status for everyone.** The owner, the merchandiser and the floor manager all see the same thing, on their phones.
- **Alerts before trouble.** An order whose QC hasn't started three days before shipping should shout. Loudly.
- **Photos where they belong.** Sample and QC photos attached to the order. Six months later, when the buyer raises a claim, the evidence is one tap away.
- **A buyer view, if you want one.** A simple status page per order saves a lot of "any update?" emails, and buyers abroad notice when a supplier has one.
- **WhatsApp, not against it.** Your team lives on WhatsApp. The system can send updates there, instead of pretending it doesn't exist.

## Off-the-shelf ERP vs a tracking system that fits

Big ERP packages can do all of this, and for a large exporter with an IT team they can be the right call. Honestly.

For a lot of small and mid-sized manufacturers, though, a full ERP turns into the expensive kind of shelfware. Half the modules are never used, the staff go back to WhatsApp within a month, and the licence keeps renewing. A tracking system built around your actual stages is smaller, cheaper to run and far more likely to be used.

And yes, someone will offer to set this up on a cheap template for a few thousand rupees. If it's a plugin bolted onto a WordPress site, ask who updates it, where the data lives, and what happens when it breaks the week before a shipment.

## How long it takes

A focused tracking system covering orders, stages and QC can be ready in 1 to 3 weeks. Adding a buyer view, documents, reports and links to inventory or accounts takes it to 2 to 4 weeks. We start with the stages that hurt most, so your team is using something real early, not waiting for the whole thing. If you also track worker attendance, it can sit alongside an [attendance system for your factory](/blog/attendance-system-for-factory-pakistan).

## The honest tradeoff

If you ship a handful of orders a month and one person genuinely has it all under control, you don't need this yet. A well-kept shared spreadsheet is fine. Keep it.

If you're shipping dozens of orders, buyers are chasing you for updates, and your best merchandiser can't take a holiday without the place catching fire, that's the sign. The software isn't the expensive part. The late shipment is.
