Sialkot makes sportswear, activewear, gloves and uniforms for brands all over the world. Walk past enough factories and you'll see labels you recognise from shops in London and Berlin.

Now open the websites of some of those same factories.

A slider with three stock photos. A product page that says "High Quality Track Suit" and nothing else. An email address at a free webmail service. And a contact form that, on a bad day, sends inquiries to nobody at all.

The factory is world-class. The website makes it look like a middleman with a printer. This post is about closing that gap, and what an **apparel export website in Sialkot** should actually show a buyer.

## What a buyer is doing on your site

An importer doesn't browse like a shopper. They're shortlisting. They have five or ten supplier tabs open and they're looking for reasons to close yours. In rough order, they're checking:

1. **Are you a real manufacturer?** Factory photos, capabilities, certifications.
2. **Do you make what I need?** Clear categories and real product specs.
3. **Can I order at my volume?** MOQ and how pricing works.
4. **Can you put my brand on it?** Private label.
5. **Will it arrive right and on time?** QC and shipping terms.
6. **Can I get a quote without a week of emails?** A proper inquiry form.

Answer those on the site and you get serious inquiries. Skip them and you get "plz send price list" from someone you'll never hear from again.

## Specs, not adjectives

"Premium quality, soft fabric, best price" describes every track suit ever made. Buyers want numbers:

- **Fabric and blend.** Cotton/polyester fleece, nylon/elastane, French terry.
- **GSM.** The fabric weight. A buyer selling heavyweight tees needs to know yours is 240 GSM, not "thick".
- **Size range.** XS to 2XL? Kids? Plus sizes?
- **Customisation.** Printing, embroidery, woven labels, custom colours.

On our [Meridian concept store](/work/meridian-retail-wholesale-store), every product card shows the GSM and fabric blend, and buyers can filter the whole catalogue by fabric weight. That one filter saves a buyer more time than any slider ever has.

## MOQ and volume pricing, in public

This is where most Sialkot export sites go quiet, and buyers notice.

You don't have to publish every price. But you should publish your **MOQ** (minimum order quantity) and make it clear that pricing improves with volume. Meridian goes further: switch the site to Wholesale and every product shows its MOQ and three volume price bands. Switch back to Retail and the same product shows a single-piece price.

Why bother? Because the buyer who can see the numbers tonight sends a real inquiry tomorrow. The buyer who can't sees the next supplier's site instead.

## Private label, QC and shipping terms, in plain words

These three pages do the job of your best sales call.

**Private label.** Say clearly that you produce under the buyer's brand, what you can customise, and what the minimums are for custom work. Add a private-label checkbox to your quote form so the buyer tells you upfront.

**Quality control.** Explain how orders are inspected before they ship. If you work to an inspection standard such as AQL 2.5 (which Meridian's wholesale page uses as its example), name it. Buyers know what it means, and it tells them you do too.

**Shipping terms.** FOB means you deliver the goods onto the ship at your port, and the buyer handles the rest. CIF means you also pay for freight and insurance to their port. Say which ones you offer. That single line answers a question that otherwise costs a day of back-and-forth.

Some Sialkot manufacturers now sell their own brand online as well as making for others. You don't need two websites for that. One catalogue can show piece prices and a normal cart to shoppers, and MOQ pricing and a quote form to importers. Prices can switch between currencies, including USD, EUR, GBP, AED and PKR. That's the exact idea [Meridian](/work/meridian-retail-wholesale-store) was built to demonstrate, and you can click through it.

## A quick checklist for your current site

Open your website on your phone and check:

- Does it load in a couple of seconds on mobile data?
- Does every product show fabric, GSM and size range?
- Is your MOQ written down anywhere?
- Is there a quote form asking for quantity and private-label needs?
- Do you explain QC and FOB / CIF?
- Is the email address on your own domain?
- Does Google show your product pages, not just your homepage?

If you ticked fewer than five, the site is costing you inquiries.
