Every factory in Pakistan has an attendance management system. Some of them even work.

The rest are a register at the gate, signed by whoever got there first, on behalf of everyone who didn't. Your night shift has a perfect attendance record. Your night shift was also, according to the register, present on a public holiday when the factory was locked.

Impressive commitment. Slightly suspicious.

Then payroll day arrives, and somebody in accounts spends two days adding up overtime from a printout, a register and a WhatsApp voice note that begins "Sir, actually what happened was."

Let's fix the system, not the voice notes.

## What an attendance management system in Pakistan has to handle

A factory isn't an office with nine-to-five staff and a coffee machine. The system has to deal with:

- **Shifts**, including the ones that cross midnight, so a worker doesn't come in on Monday and leave on Tuesday as two half days.
- **Overtime**, at different rates, approved by a supervisor before it counts, not after the money's gone.
- **Late arrivals and short leaves**, with rules that are the same for everyone, written down somewhere other than the manager's mood.
- **Contract and daily-wage workers** who aren't on the permanent payroll but still need paying correctly.
- **Payroll day**, which is the entire point. Punches are just data. Wages are what people care about.

If a system handles the gate but not those, you've bought a very expensive clock.

## The biometric machine that everyone's cousin can't punch in for

Proxy punching (one worker signing in for an absent friend) is the main reason factories buy biometric devices in the first place. It's hard to sign in for a friend when the machine wants their face.

Fingerprint scanners are cheap and common, and they work well in offices. On a factory floor they hit a predictable problem: worn, cut or greasy fingers. Stitching, leather work, metal polishing. The scanner says "try again", the queue backs up at 8:58, and someone gets marked late for having a job that uses their hands.

Face recognition devices avoid this, and they're contactless, which matters at a gate used by 400 people. Many factories now go face-first and keep fingerprint or a card as a fallback.

Either way, here's the uncomfortable truth: **the machine is the easy part.** The hard part is everything that happens to the punches after they leave the machine.

## Where most attendance setups actually break

Most factories that "have biometric" still have a person who exports a report from the device every month, opens it in Excel, and manually works out who was late, who did overtime, and who was on approved leave.

That person is the real attendance system. The machine is their assistant.

The report doesn't know about shifts that cross midnight. It doesn't know Friday is different. It doesn't know the supervisor approved Kashif's overtime on paper, which is now in a drawer, under another drawer.

Good software sits between the device and payroll. It pulls punches automatically, applies your shift rules, flags anything odd (a punch-in with no punch-out, overtime that wasn't approved), and hands payroll a clean number. Accounts stops being a detective agency.

## Mobile app attendance: when it makes sense

An app is a great fit for people who aren't at one gate: field staff, drivers, sales teams, supervisors moving between units, workers at a client's site.

The app can record location at check-in and take a photo, so "I was at the site, the signal was bad" becomes something you can check instead of something you have to believe. It can also work offline and sync later, because in our experience the signal is always bad exactly when someone's late.

We've built this kind of thing before, just pointed at different work. MainXpert, a [vehicle inspection app](/work/mainxpert), walks users through a guided photo workflow so every report has the evidence it needs. And the [operations platform we run for a UK surveying firm](/work/surveyor-job-management-system) has contractors fill a six-step site visit form, with photos and sign-off, that saves as they go and locks read-only once it's submitted. Their rule is "No photos, no payment." It's a good rule. It works for attendance too.

For a fixed gate with hundreds of workers arriving at once, though, don't make everyone open an app. A device at the entrance is faster. Use both: devices at the gate, the app for everyone who isn't there.

## Off-the-shelf or custom?

Honest answer: **if you're a small unit with one shift and straightforward pay, buy a standard device with its bundled software.** It'll do the job, and you'll be running this week.

Custom starts to earn its keep when:

- You run multiple shifts, multiple units, or multiple sites, and the bundled software assumes one office.
- Your overtime, leave and deduction rules need a spreadsheet to work out, every single month.
- You want attendance to feed straight into payroll, so wages are calculated, not assembled.
- You want WhatsApp alerts to supervisors when someone's missing at shift start, and to the owner when overtime goes past budget.
