An Easypaisa scam almost never hacks anything. It doesn't need to. It just asks you, very politely, for the keys. "Sir, your account will be blocked in 30 minutes, please tell me the code we just sent." And then, a few seconds later, your balance is zero.

Here's the short answer. Easypaisa, JazzCash, your bank and every government programme will never ask for your PIN or the OTP sent to your phone. Anyone who does is a scammer. If you've already shared it, call your wallet's helpline immediately, then report it to the NCCIA.

JazzCash scams work exactly the same way, so everything here applies to both.

## The Easypaisa and JazzCash scams you'll hear about

### "Your account will be blocked"

A call or SMS says your wallet is being blocked, your biometric verification has expired, or there's a problem with your SIM. To fix it, they need the code that's about to arrive on your phone.

That code isn't for verification. It's for logging in to your account, or approving a transaction, from their phone.

### "You've won a prize"

A car, a motorbike, cash, a lucky draw from a TV show or a telecom company. Just pay a small "tax" first, or share the code to receive it. The only real winner is the person on the other end.

### Fake government support

Messages and calls pretending to be from BISP, Ehsaas or other support programmes, telling you money is waiting. To release it, pay a fee or share a code. These target the people who can least afford to lose anything, which tells you everything about the people running them.

### "I sent money to you by mistake"

You get an SMS that looks like a payment notification, then a desperate call: "Bhai, I sent 5,000 to the wrong number, please send it back." The SMS is fake. No money arrived. Check your balance in the app itself, never the SMS. If a real payment arrived by mistake, the wallet provider can reverse it. You don't need to send anything.

### The fake officer

Someone claiming to be from the FIA, the police, the PTA or a bank's fraud team. Your account is "linked to a crime", and you need to verify your details to clear your name. Real officials don't verify anything by asking for your PIN.

## How to protect your wallet

- **Never share your PIN or OTP.** Not with "Easypaisa", "JazzCash", a bank, the police or your cousin.
- **Check balances in the app,** not in SMS messages.
- **Hang up on urgency.** "30 minutes or your account closes" is a script, not a deadline.
- **Call back on the official number,** from the app or website, if you're unsure.
- **Lock your phone and app** with a PIN or fingerprint, and don't write your PIN on the phone cover. (Yes, people do.)
- **Talk to your parents.** Older relatives and first-time wallet users are the main targets. A two-minute conversation now saves a very bad afternoon later.

Easypaisa publishes its own [fraud awareness guidance](https://easypaisa.com.pk/?p=1960), and it's worth a read.

## How to report an Easypaisa or JazzCash scam

Speed matters. Wallet providers can sometimes block a transaction or the receiving account if you call quickly.

1. **Call your wallet's helpline immediately.** Easypaisa lists **3737** for Telenor users, and the numbers for other networks are in the app and on its website. For JazzCash, use the helpline listed in the JazzCash app or on its official website.
2. **File a complaint with the NCCIA,** the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency, which took over cybercrime from the FIA's cyber wing. Its helpline is **1799**, and there's an online complaint portal too.
3. **Report the scam number** to your mobile network so it can be looked into.
4. **Keep the evidence.** Screenshots of messages, the caller's number, transaction IDs and the time it happened.
