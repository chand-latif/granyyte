"How much did it cost?"

"Fifteen thousand."

"Who built it?"

"An expert."

Sialkot has a lot of website experts. Most of them, it turns out, build the same website. So let's open the box on cheap website development in Sialkot and look at what a 10,000 to 20,000 rupee site usually contains. Then, because it would be unfair not to, we'll talk about when buying one is a perfectly sensible decision.

## Unboxing a 15,000 rupee website

Here's what's typically inside. Not every cheap site has all of these. Most have a few.

### A theme that has been to a lot of weddings

The site is a theme from a marketplace, bought once and installed for client after client. Your logo goes in the corner, your phone number goes in the header, the stock photo of a smiling man in a hard hat stays exactly where it was. Sometimes the demo text survives too, and somewhere in your footer a Latin paragraph is still quietly saying *lorem ipsum dolor sit amet*.

That's why so many Sialkot business sites look like the same site wearing a different hat. Because they are.

### Thirty plugins and a prayer

Need a contact form? Plugin. Slider? Plugin. WhatsApp button? Plugin. Speed? Another plugin, to undo the damage done by the other plugins.

Each plugin is code written by a stranger. Each one needs updates. On a typical cheap build, nobody is scheduled to do those updates, so the site slowly becomes a museum of old software with known holes in it. WordPress's own [hardening guide](https://developer.wordpress.org/advanced-administration/security/hardening/) spends a lot of time on exactly this, and almost nobody at the cheap end of the market has read it.

### The front door, wide open

Visit `yoursite.com/wp-admin` on many cheap sites and you'll find the login page right where every bot on the internet expects it. Username: `admin`. Password: hopefully not the business name followed by 123.

Bots don't care that you're a small surgical instruments supplier in Sialkot. They try every door on every site, all day, and they're very patient.

### SEO, in the sense that the word appears in the proposal

Every page is titled "Home". There's no sitemap. The images are 6 MB each, straight off a phone, so the page takes long enough to load on 4G that the visitor has time to reconsider their life choices. Google can technically find the site. It just has no reason to show it to anyone.

### Hosting in someone else's name

The domain and hosting are often registered to the developer. That's fine right up until you stop working together, at which point your website becomes a hostage. It's surprisingly common for a business to rebuild a site from scratch simply because nobody can get the password back.

### The developer, now unavailable

Something breaks six months later. The number goes to voicemail, or the reply is "that will be extra", or the person who built it has moved on to a job in Lahore. Nobody is accountable, because nothing written ever said they would be.

## Why it's cheap (it's not a scam, it's maths)

To be fair to the people selling these, the price isn't a con. It's arithmetic. If you buy a theme once and deliver it to forty clients, each delivery takes a day or two, so 15,000 rupees is a decent day's work.

What that price can't include is everything that takes engineering time: designing for your actual buyer, writing clean code, setting up security, making the site fast, structuring it for Google, and someone answering the phone when it breaks. Those things aren't extras. They're the job. The cheap version just leaves them out and hopes you won't notice for a while.

## When a cheap website is honestly fine

We'd rather lose a sale than tell you something untrue, so here it is. A 15,000 rupee website can be the right call when:

- You need a simple online presence so customers can find your address and phone number.
- Nearly all your business comes from people who already know you.
- You aren't relying on it to impress buyers abroad or rank on Google.
- You're fine replacing it in a couple of years.

If that's you, buy the cheap one, ask for the domain in your own name, and spend the money you saved on something that grows your business. Genuinely.

## When it's going to cost you more than it saved

The cheap site gets expensive the day you need it to *do* something.

If you export, a buyer in Germany or the US will look you up before they reply to your quote. They'll judge you in seconds. A slow site that looks like three other suppliers' sites doesn't say "reliable factory". It says "middleman with a stock photo".

If you want Google traffic, a site Google can't properly read won't get it, no matter how many times "SEO" appears in the invoice.

And if it gets hacked, you'll pay for the cleanup, lose whatever rankings you had, and possibly end up with a warning in Google's results telling people your site is unsafe. That's a rough day for 15,000 rupees of savings.

## Eight questions to ask before you buy any website

Ask whoever you're talking to. Ask us too.

1. Is this a theme? If yes, which one, and how many other clients have it?
2. How many plugins will it use, and who updates them, and how often?
3. Will the domain and hosting be in my name?
4. Do I get the full source code and every login at the end?
5. How fast will the homepage load on a phone? (Test their past work in Google's [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/).)
6. Will each page have its own title and description, and will you set up Search Console?
7. What happens if the site breaks in month seven?
8. Can I see three sites you built that look nothing alike?

If the answers are vague, you've found out what you're buying.
