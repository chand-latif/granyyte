WhatsApp scams are what happens when the app everybody trusts meets the people nobody should. Your family group, your work chats, your kids' school updates, all in one place. Scammers love it for exactly the same reason you do.

Here's the short answer. Most WhatsApp scams come down to three tricks: pretending to be someone you know, getting your 6-digit code, or adding you to a group that promises easy money. Turn on two-step verification, never share your code, and check any money request through another channel. That stops most of them cold.

## The WhatsApp scams you'll actually see

### 1. "Hi Mum, this is my new number"

A message from an unknown number. "Hi Mum, I dropped my phone, this is my new number." A bit of chat, then the ask: a bill they need paying today, because their banking app isn't working on the new phone.

It works because it's ordinary. Phones do break. Kids do need money. The tell is that the new number can't take a call ("the mic's broken too"), and the payment has to go to someone else's account.

**The fix:** call the old number. If your child answers, sitting perfectly well on the sofa with their perfectly working phone, you have your answer.

### 2. The 6-digit code takeover

You get a text with a WhatsApp code you didn't ask for. Then a message, often from a friend's hacked account: "Sorry, I sent my code to your number by mistake, can you send it back?"

Don't. That code registers your number on their phone. They take over your WhatsApp and start messaging all your contacts, as you, asking for money. One account becomes fifty victims by lunchtime.

Nobody legitimate ever needs your code. Not a friend. Not "WhatsApp support". Not your cousin's new business partner.

### 3. Job and "task" offers

"Hello, we're hiring part-time. Earn from home by liking videos or rating products." The first few tasks pay small amounts, so it feels real. Then you need to "deposit" money to get bigger tasks. The deposits never come back. We cover this one properly in [fake job offer scams](/blog/fake-job-offer-scams).

### 4. Investment and trading groups

You're added to a group full of excited people posting screenshots of huge profits. A "mentor" offers to help you trade. The app they send you shows your money growing. The withdrawal button, curiously, never quite works. Everyone in that group except you may be part of the act.

### 5. Prizes, deliveries and wallet messages

"You've won", "your parcel is held", "your account will be blocked". A link, a form, a request for your details or a PIN. In Pakistan this often impersonates mobile wallets, which we cover in [Easypaisa and JazzCash scams](/blog/easypaisa-jazzcash-scams).

## How to secure your WhatsApp in five minutes

Do these now, while you're thinking about it:

- **Turn on two-step verification.** Settings, then Account, then Two-step verification. Even if someone gets your code, they'll need this as well.
- **Never share your 6-digit code.** Ever. With anyone. That's the whole rule.
- **Check linked devices.** Settings, then Linked devices. Remove anything you don't recognise.
- **Tighten privacy.** Choose who can add you to groups, and who can see your profile photo and last seen.
- **Be wary of links and QR codes** from people you don't know, or from people you do know who suddenly sound unlike themselves.

WhatsApp's own [security page](https://www.whatsapp.com/security) explains the features in more detail.

## How to report a scammer on WhatsApp

Open the chat, tap the contact or group name, and choose **Report**. That sends recent messages to WhatsApp and lets you block them in the same step. Reporting matters: it's how scam accounts get banned before they reach the next person.

If you've lost money, WhatsApp can't get it back for you. Call your bank or wallet provider first, then report it:

- **US:** [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/)
- **UK:** [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/)
- **Pakistan:** your bank or wallet first, then the NCCIA (National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency)

Our full step-by-step is in [what to do if you've been scammed](/blog/what-to-do-if-you-have-been-scammed).

## If your account was taken over

1. **Reinstall WhatsApp and register your number** with the code sent to your phone. That logs the scammer out.
2. **Turn on two-step verification** straight away.
3. **Warn your contacts** by SMS, a call, or a family member's phone. The scammer will already be asking them for money.
4. **Contact WhatsApp support** from inside the app if you can't get back in.
