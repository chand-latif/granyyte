School management software in Sialkot exists. Plenty of schools still run on a fee register, a stapled stack of vouchers, and a parents' WhatsApp group that has, at some point, become sentient.

You know the group. One announcement about Friday's early closing, then 63 replies saying "ok", 9 asking "what time?", and one parent forwarding a recipe.

Meanwhile the accounts office is matching bank slips against a register by hand, the defaulter list lives in one teacher's head, and attendance is marked on paper and then typed into Excel by somebody who'd rather be doing anything else.

None of this is a technology problem. It's a "we've always done it like this" problem wearing a technology costume. Let's take the costume off.

## What school management software in Sialkot actually needs to do

Forget the brochure with 47 modules. A school needs a short list done properly.

### Fee vouchers that collect themselves

This is where schools lose the most money and the most sleep. Vouchers get printed, sent home, lost in a school bag next to a banana, and then paid at a bank with a slip that somebody has to match to a student by hand.

Good software generates vouchers per student, applies sibling discounts and scholarships automatically, and tracks who's paid. Better software puts a payment reference or QR code on the voucher so parents pay from their phone. Pakistan's instant payment system, [Raast](https://www.sbp.org.pk/dfs/Raast.html), plus bank and wallet bill payments, makes this realistic now, not a 2035 fantasy.

The payoff is a defaulter list that's always current. Not "Sir, I'll check and tell you." Current.

### Attendance that isn't a paper exercise

Paper attendance is marked, then copied, then summarised, then lost. Three jobs, zero useful output.

Teachers should mark attendance on a phone in under a minute, and the parent of an absent child should know by 9am, automatically. That single alert solves more problems than any dashboard. It also ends the "but he left for school this morning" conversation at a much more useful time of day.

### Results without the photocopier

Teachers enter marks once. The software calculates grades and positions and produces the report card. Nobody spends a weekend retyping marks into a template, and nobody's position gets miscalculated because a row shifted in Excel.

(It always shifts. Excel does it out of spite.)

### Reaching parents where they already are

Parents read WhatsApp. They do not read your website, they do not check the portal you launched in 2019, and they definitely do not read the circular in the school bag next to the banana.

So fee reminders, absence alerts and results go out on WhatsApp, with SMS or email as backup. We already run automated WhatsApp and email notifications in a [live operations platform for a UK firm](/work/surveyor-job-management-system), where the right person gets the right link the moment something's ready. A fee reminder is the same plumbing pointed at a different audience.

## The features that sound great in the demo

Every vendor demo has them. A 3D campus map. AI that predicts which students will fail. A "smart" timetable that needs four days of setup to save forty minutes a year.

Ask one question about each: *who will use this every single week?* If the honest answer is nobody, it's decoration. You're paying for decoration.

## Ready-made or built for you?

The honest answer first, because it might save you money: **most single-campus schools should buy a good ready-made system.** Fees, attendance, results and parent messages are well-solved problems, and several packages sold in Pakistan handle them. Pick one with local support, try it for a term, and only move if it genuinely doesn't fit.

Custom starts making sense when:

- You run several branches with different fee structures, and the package handles "a school", singular.
- Your fee rules (installments, sibling and staff discounts, scholarships, late fees) need workarounds in every package you've tried.
- You want your own branded parent app on the App Store and Google Play, not a vendor's app with your logo in the corner.
- You want to own your data and stop paying a per-student monthly fee that grows with every admission.

That last point quietly matters. A per-student fee feels small when you have 300 students and stings when you have 1,500. ## The questions to ask any vendor, including us

- Where is our data stored, and can we export all of it whenever we want?
- What happens when the internet goes down mid-attendance?
- Can a teacher see the fee ledger? (The answer should be no.)
- Who do we call when vouchers don't generate the night before they're due, and do they pick up?
- What does it cost in year three, not just year one?

A vendor who answers all five plainly is worth talking to. One who answers with a brochure is not.
