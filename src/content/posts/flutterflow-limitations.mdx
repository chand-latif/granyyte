FlutterFlow limitations are a strange thing to write about when we hold the official FlutterFlow certification and have shipped apps with it on both stores. It's a bit like a car dealer listing the problems with the car. But here we are, because the honest version wins more clients than the brochure does.

Short answer first: FlutterFlow is excellent for apps built from standard pieces, and it starts to fight you once your app stops being standard. In 2026, with AI coding agents writing most of the code, that line has moved. A new app meant to last for years now usually leans to plain Flutter.

FlutterFlow is still a good tool. It just isn't the answer to everything, and anyone telling you it is is selling FlutterFlow hours.

## Where FlutterFlow is genuinely great

Give it credit where it's earned:

- **Standard apps, fast.** Logins, lists, forms, profiles, a Firebase or Supabase backend. FlutterFlow builds this quickly and well.
- **Real Flutter underneath.** It isn't a toy runtime. It generates a Flutter app, and on paid plans you can [export the code](https://docs.flutterflow.io/) and own it outright.
- **Validating an idea.** When the goal is "get something in front of users before the money runs out", it's hard to beat.

We built [AVC Dealer](/work/avc-dealer), a dealer app covering 50+ auction locations across 25 US states, with Flutter and FlutterFlow. It works. Nobody using it is wondering how the screens were made.

## FlutterFlow limitations we actually run into

Not a list copied from a forum. The ones that show up on real projects:

### 1. Complex logic lives in custom code anyway

Every serious app has some logic that doesn't fit a visual action chain. Pricing rules, multi-step validation, anything with lots of branches. In FlutterFlow that goes into custom code. Add enough custom code and you're writing a Flutter app with a visual builder sitting awkwardly on top.

### 2. Unusual UI takes negotiation

Standard layouts are quick. A design that does something the widget panel didn't anticipate turns into a back-and-forth between the designer, the developer and the builder. The builder usually wins. Your design usually loses a little.

### 3. Device-level and heavy features

Bluetooth hardware, long background tasks, custom audio engines, AR. These were never FlutterFlow's strength, and they push you to custom code or plain Flutter. (They're also where native development still earns its keep. We compare all three in [FlutterFlow vs native development](/blog/flutterflow-vs-native-development).)

### 4. Teams and history

Code has decades of tooling for several people working on the same thing: branches, reviews, diffs you can read. A visual builder has improved here, but reviewing a change in a visual project is still harder than reading a pull request.

### 5. The one-way door

You can export the code and keep editing it by hand. Once you do, those hand edits don't flow back into the visual builder. That's fine if you planned it. It's a nasty surprise if you didn't.

## Why AI agents moved the line

This is the newer argument, and it's one Chand makes bluntly: for a brand-new project, starting in low-code is now often the wrong bet.

Two reasons. First, an AI coding agent working in plain code can do almost anything the language allows. An agent working inside a low-code platform is still capped by what the platform allows. Second, the money: you'd be paying for the agent and the platform, two subscriptions instead of one, to get the less flexible result.

Low-code made building easier and brought its own problems. If you want something that scales, you end up getting your hands dirty anyway. Agents just made getting your hands dirty a lot cheaper. We wrote more on what agents change, and what they don't, in [AI coding agents won't save a project nobody understands](/blog/ai-coding-agents-software-development).

## When we still recommend FlutterFlow

- **You're already on it and it works.** Keep it. Migrating costs real money and delivers nothing your users can see. Move only when the platform is actively blocking you, and then do it in steps.
- **Your team maintains it.** If the people looking after the app afterwards work in FlutterFlow and not in code, that matters more than our preference.
- **A quick validation build.** One core flow, a few weeks, a real answer about whether anyone wants it. FlutterFlow is still very good at that.

And when we'd point you to plain Flutter: a new product you expect to grow for years, lots of custom logic, several developers, or features near the device. Either way, it's still one codebase for iOS and Android, so you're not paying for the app twice.

## The honest bottom line

FlutterFlow is a good tool with a ceiling. The ceiling is higher than its critics say and lower than its fans hope. Know where yours is before you start, not in month four when the custom code folder is bigger than the app.
