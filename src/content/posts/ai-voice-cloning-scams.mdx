An AI voice cloning scam works like this: your phone rings, and it's your son. Or it sounds exactly like him. He's crying, there's been an accident, he needs money right now, and please don't tell Dad.

It isn't him. It's a scammer with a few seconds of his voice from a video he posted, and a piece of software that's cheap and easy to find.

Here's the short answer. Don't trust a voice on its own any more. If a call from someone you love asks for urgent money, hang up and call them back on the number you already have. And agree a family safe word today, before you need it.

## How the AI voice cloning scam works

The scam isn't new. "Grandma, it's me, I'm in trouble" has been around for decades. What changed is the voice.

1. **They get a sample.** A short clip is enough, the [FTC warns](https://consumer.ftc.gov/scams/family-emergency-scams). A video on Instagram, a voice note, a TikTok, a wedding speech on YouTube.
2. **They clone it.** Voice cloning tools turn that clip into a voice that can say anything.
3. **They call with an emergency.** An accident, an arrest, a kidnapping, a hospital bill. Always urgent. Always secret.
4. **They hand over to an "official".** A "lawyer", "police officer" or "doctor" takes the phone and explains how to pay.
5. **They want untraceable money.** Wire transfers, gift cards, crypto, or a courier coming to your door.

The script is designed to do one thing: stop you from thinking for ten minutes. The emergency stops you calling anyone. The secrecy stops you asking anyone. The urgency stops you checking anything. Scammers aren't geniuses. They've just read the same psychology textbook more carefully than the rest of us.

## The warning signs, even when the voice is perfect

You can't rely on hearing the fake. Some clones are rough. Some are very good. Listen for the situation instead:

- **Urgent money, right now.** Real emergencies have hospitals and police stations that will still be there in ten minutes.
- **"Don't tell anyone."** The single biggest red flag. Your family wants you to tell people when they're in trouble.
- **Odd payment methods.** Gift cards, crypto, wire transfers or a courier collecting cash. No hospital takes iTunes cards.
- **A number you don't recognise.** "My phone broke, I'm using a friend's." Convenient.
- **Short, emotional, and then someone else.** Clones are easiest for a few panicked sentences. Then the "official" takes over.

## How to protect your family from voice cloning scams

### 1. Agree a family safe word

Pick a word or phrase together, in person. Something nobody would guess and nobody has ever posted. "Blue pineapple" works. Your dog's name doesn't, because it's in forty of your photos.

If anyone calls in trouble, ask for the word. A real son knows it. A clone just gets confused, and suddenly the line is very bad.

### 2. Hang up and call back

Every time. On the number you already have saved, not the one that called you. If you can't reach them, call someone who's with them, or another family member. It feels rude to hang up on someone crying. It's much ruder to send your savings to a stranger.

### 3. Ask something only they'd know

Not "what's your birthday", which is on Facebook. Something like what you had for dinner last time they visited, or what they called their first teacher.

### 4. Share less voice online

You don't have to vanish from the internet. But think about public videos of family members speaking at length, especially older relatives and children. Private accounts help.

### 5. Talk to your parents and grandparents

They're the classic target. Have the conversation now, calmly, over tea, not after a call. Show them this page if it helps. We also wrote a guide on [how to tell if a text is a scam](/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-text-is-a-scam), which uses the same stop-and-check habit.

## The same trick, aimed at your business

The same trick works on businesses. An accounts clerk gets a call, or a voice note, that sounds exactly like the owner asking for an urgent transfer to a new supplier. Same rules apply: a callback on a known number, and a second person to approve any unusual payment. If the boss is annoyed you checked, the boss can be annoyed. The money stays put.

## If it already happened

Move fast, and don't blame yourself. These calls are built to fool careful people.

- **Call your bank or payment provider now.** Ask them to stop or reverse the payment.
- **Report it.** In the US, at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/). In the UK, through [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/). In Pakistan, contact your bank first, then the NCCIA.
- **Keep everything.** Call logs, numbers, messages, payment receipts.
