Your phone buzzes. "Your parcel could not be delivered. Please confirm your address and pay a £1.45 redelivery fee." You're not even expecting a parcel. But you might be. You did order *something* last week, didn't you?

That tiny moment of doubt is the whole business model. Scam texts don't need to fool you forever, just for the ten seconds it takes to tap a link. So here's **how to tell if a text is a scam**, quickly, before the ten seconds are up.

We built [SCAMalicious](/work/scamalicious), an anti-scam app with more than 5,000 downloads on Google Play, so we spend a slightly unhealthy amount of time thinking about how scams work. This is the short version.

## The 9 signs a text is a scam

You don't need all nine. Two or three together should make you stop.

### 1. It's urgent for no good reason

"Your account will be suspended today." "Final notice." "Act within 24 hours." Pressure is the scammer's favourite tool, because people who feel rushed don't check. Genuine companies give you time.

### 2. There's a link you weren't expecting

Links in texts are how most scams start. A real bank or delivery company is happy for you to ignore the link and open their official app instead. A scammer is not.

### 3. The link looks almost right

`royalmail-redelivery-uk.com`. `amaz0n-support.help`. Scammers register addresses that look right at a glance. If it isn't the company's normal web address, treat it as fake.

### 4. It asks for a code

"We've sent you a code, please reply with it to verify." That code is usually the key to *your* account, and the scammer is trying to log in as you right now. Never share a one-time code with anyone who contacts you.

### 5. It asks for a small payment

£1.45, $2.99, Rs 150. The fee is small so you don't think twice. The real target is the card details you type in to pay it.

### 6. It's a prize you didn't enter

You've won a phone, a voucher, a holiday. You've never entered anything. Nobody gives away iPhones to random numbers, though we admire the optimism.

### 7. It's "the bank", but vague

Real bank messages usually avoid links altogether and tell you to call the number on the back of your card. Scam ones say "Dear customer", mention "unusual activity" and point you to a link or a number they supply.

### 8. "Hi Mum, this is my new number"

A family member texting from a new number, phone broken, needs money urgently for something embarrassing. Call the old number, or ask something only they'd know. The real one will understand.

### 9. Something just feels off

Odd spelling, a strange time of night, a tone that doesn't sound like the company. Your instincts are data. Listen to them.

## What to do with a scam text

**Don't reply**, not even with "STOP". A reply tells the sender your number is live.

**Don't click.** If you think the message might be real, open the company's official app or type its web address yourself.

**Report it**, then delete it:

| Where you are | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| UK | Forward the text free to **7726** (SPAM). If you lost money or shared details, report to [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/) (England, Wales, Northern Ireland) or Police Scotland on 101 |
| US | Report at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) and forward to **7726** with most carriers. Online money losses can also go to [IC3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/) |
| Pakistan | Complain to the **NCCIA** (National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency), which replaced the FIA's cybercrime wing. Call your bank or wallet first if money moved |
| WhatsApp, Telegram and other apps | Use the app's own "Report" and "Block" options. App messages usually can't be forwarded to 7726 |

The UK's 7726 service is explained in detail by [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/scam-calls-and-messages/what-to-do-about-a-scam-call-text-or-message), and the FTC keeps a good general guide on [how to avoid a scam](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-scam).

## If you already clicked

Deep breath. Clicking a link on its own often does nothing. What matters is what happened next:

- **You only opened the page and closed it.** Usually fine. Don't go back, and keep an eye out for follow-up messages.
- **You typed a password.** Change it now, and anywhere else you use the same one. Turn on two-step verification.
- **You entered card or bank details.** Call your bank using the number on your card, tell them exactly what happened, and ask them to block the card.
- **You shared a one-time code.** Contact that company or bank immediately. Someone may be trying to get into your account.
- **You installed something.** Uninstall it, run your phone's security check, and change important passwords from a different device.

We wrote a full guide on [what to do if you've been scammed](/blog/what-to-do-if-you-have-been-scammed) for the worse days.

## A two-minute habit that beats every scam text

Here's the rule that would stop almost all of them: **never act on a link or number inside a text. Go to the company yourself.** Open the app, type the address, call the number on your card. If the message was real, it'll still be true when you get there. If it was a scam, it simply stops working.
