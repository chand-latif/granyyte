Choosing how to build your mobile app is the single most expensive decision you'll make before writing a line of code. And most founders get sold on "native" because it sounds serious, then pay for it in months of extra time and thousands of extra dollars, for a result their users can't tell apart.

Here's the honest breakdown from a team that ships with FlutterFlow, Flutter, and native tooling, and holds an official FlutterFlow certification.

## The short version

**Native means building the same app twice.** Swift for iOS, Kotlin for Android. Two codebases, two sets of bugs, two timelines, one budget paying for all of it.

**Flutter and FlutterFlow build it once,** using a single codebase that runs natively on both platforms. Same product, roughly **three times less time and money**, and you still own real, production-grade code.

For the vast majority of apps, that's the whole decision.

## The three paths

**Native development** means two separate apps in two languages. Maximum control, and maximum duplication. Every screen, every fix, and every feature is built twice.

**Flutter** is Google's cross-platform framework: one Dart codebase compiled to native code on both platforms. Near-native performance, one team, one timeline.

**FlutterFlow** sits on top of Flutter as a visual development platform that generates real Flutter code. It's not a toy website builder. It exports production Dart code you fully own, and it's where the biggest speed gains come from.

## Speed and cost: the real numbers

| Approach | Relative cost & time | Typical delivery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native (iOS + Android) | ~3x | 1-3 months |
| Flutter | Baseline | 2-4 weeks |
| FlutterFlow | Lowest | 2-4 weeks |

The gap comes from one place: **duplication.** Native means everything is built twice. Flutter eliminates the duplication, and FlutterFlow additionally eliminates most of the boilerplate. That's why we can deliver most apps in **2-4 weeks** instead of the months native takes, for the same product at a fraction of the time and cost.

## When native is actually worth it

Native still wins in a few specific scenarios:

- **Heavy platform integration**, like advanced ARKit and ML features, complex background processing, or deep hardware access
- **Games and graphics-intensive apps**, though engines like Unity matter more here than the native-vs-cross-platform debate
- **Apps where 16ms frame budgets genuinely matter**, such as high-frequency trading UIs and professional audio tools

If your app isn't in one of these categories, and roughly 90% of business apps aren't, native means paying triple for a difference your users will never perceive.

## When Flutter is the sweet spot

Flutter is our default for funded products with real custom logic:

- One codebase, native performance, and full access to hand-written custom code
- A mature ecosystem where payments, maps, notifications, and analytics are all solved problems
- Easy hiring and handover, since Flutter is one of the most popular frameworks in the world

## When FlutterFlow wins

FlutterFlow is the fastest legitimate route from idea to app store, and where we hold official certification:

- **MVPs and validation**, getting you in front of real users in weeks rather than months
- **Standard app patterns** like feeds, profiles, chat, bookings, marketplaces, and dashboards
- **Budget-conscious founders**, because the savings are real and the output is real Flutter code

The detail most people miss: **FlutterFlow is not a dead end.** Because it exports genuine Flutter code, you can eject into hand-written Flutter whenever your product outgrows visual development. Your investment carries forward, and nothing gets thrown away.

That last row is the one that matters. We've shipped [Bilge AI](/work/bilge-ai), a multi-model AI app used by more than 15,000 people in Turkey, and [Zwipe](/work/zwipe), a car marketplace in the UAE, on Flutter. Nobody downloading them checks what language they were written in. They check whether the app works, and whether it's fast.

## 2026 update: FlutterFlow or plain Flutter for a new app?

We said above that FlutterFlow isn't a dead end, because the code exports. That's still true. What's changed is everything around it.

AI coding agents got very good, very fast. Point one at plain Flutter code and it can do anything the code can do. Point the same agent at a low-code platform and it can only do what the platform allows, and now you're paying for the platform and the agent. Two subscriptions, one ceiling.

The platforms know it. They're adding AI features as fast as they can, which mostly adds another layer of setup between you and your app.

So here's where we've landed, as people who are certified in FlutterFlow and still use it:

- **Already on FlutterFlow?** Stay. Migrating a working app costs real money and rarely pays for itself. We'll extend it with custom code where it needs more.
- **Validating an idea fast, or your team wants to edit screens visually?** FlutterFlow still earns its place.
- **Starting a product you expect to grow for years?** We'll usually recommend plain Flutter from day one. With a good engineer and a good agent, it's no longer the slow option.

One catch, and it's a big one. An agent is only as good as the developer's understanding of the whole system. It fills the gaps in your context with guesses. "Use AI and skip the engineer" is how you get an app that works in the demo and nowhere else.

## Our honest recommendation

1. **Validating an idea?** FlutterFlow. Ship in 2-4 weeks, learn from real users, iterate cheap.
2. **Building a funded product with standard patterns?** FlutterFlow or Flutter, depending on complexity.
3. **Deep platform features or extreme performance needs?** Flutter with custom native modules, or fully native if you're in that rare 10%.
4. **A brand-new product built to last years?** Increasingly, plain Flutter from the start (see the 2026 update above).

The wrong answer is defaulting to native because it feels "more serious." Serious is shipping the same product in a third of the time and budget.
