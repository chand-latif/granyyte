A job offer scam is the only kind of job where you pay the company. It's quite a business model, when you think about it. No salaries, no office, no HR. Just you, a WhatsApp message and a "small registration fee".

Here's the short answer. A real job pays you. If an offer arrives out of nowhere, skips a proper interview, happens only over chat, or asks you to pay anything before you start, treat it as a scam until you've checked it independently. Most of them fail that test in about thirty seconds.

## The job offer scams doing the rounds

### The task scam

The fastest-growing one. A friendly message offers part-time work: liking videos, rating hotels, "boosting" products. The first tasks pay a little, which feels like proof. Then the system says you need to deposit money to reach the next level, or to withdraw what you've earned. Every deposit leads to another one.

The US Federal Trade Commission's data shows reports of these [task scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/08/how-spot-and-avoid-task-scams) going from almost none in 2020 to tens of thousands a few years later. It isn't a niche trick any more. It's an industry.

### The registration or training fee

"Congratulations, you're selected. Pay for registration, training, a background check or a uniform to confirm your place." The job doesn't exist. The fee is the job.

### The fake cheque

You're "hired" for remote work and sent a cheque to buy equipment from their approved supplier. You deposit it, pay the supplier, and a few days later the cheque bounces. The supplier was them. The bank wants its money back. From you.

### The overseas job

A well-paid job abroad, often in the Gulf or Europe, with visa, ticket and accommodation "arranged". All you need to pay is a processing fee. Then another. Then the agent's phone switches off. In Pakistan, check that any overseas employment promoter is properly licensed with the government before paying anything.

### The chat-only interview

The whole "interview" happens over WhatsApp or Telegram text. No call, no video, no address. Then a job offer arrives with excellent pay and asks for your bank details, ID and a copy of your passport. That's not onboarding. That's identity theft with a welcome message.

## Red flags in any job offer

- **They found you.** You didn't apply, but you've been "shortlisted".
- **Too much money for too little work.** Liking videos does not pay a manager's salary.
- **Any payment from you.** Fees, deposits, equipment, training. All of it.
- **Chat only.** No call, no office, no named manager you can look up.
- **Personal email or WhatsApp number** instead of a company domain.
- **Pressure.** "Only three places left, confirm today."
- **Odd payment methods.** Crypto, gift cards, transfers to personal accounts.

If you spot two of these, walk away. If you spot four, they probably sent the same message to ten thousand people before breakfast.

## How to check a job is real

1. **Find the company yourself.** Search for its official website. Don't use the link in the message.
2. **Contact them through their own channels.** Ask if the job and the recruiter are real. Real HR teams are used to this question.
3. **Check the email address.** `careers@company.com` is plausible. `company.hr.jobs2026@gmail.com` isn't.
4. **Look the recruiter up.** A real recruiter usually has a LinkedIn history longer than last Tuesday.
5. **Never pay to get paid.** The FTC's rule of thumb, and the simplest one on this page.

## If you've already paid

Stop. Don't send another payment, even if they promise a refund once you "complete the level" or "pay the release fee". That's the same scam, round two.

- **Call your bank or wallet provider** immediately and ask them to stop the payment.
- **Report it.** In the US, at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/). In the UK, through [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/). In Pakistan, your bank or wallet first, then the NCCIA.
- **If you shared ID or bank details,** watch your accounts closely and ask your bank about extra protection.
- **Report the account** on WhatsApp, Telegram or the job site where it started.
