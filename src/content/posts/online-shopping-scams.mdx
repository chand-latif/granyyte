There's a hoodie on Instagram. It's the brand you like, in the colour you like, at 70% off. The shop has a slick website, a countdown timer, and a little pop-up saying "Sarah from Manchester just bought this!".

There's a real chance Sarah doesn't exist, the timer resets every time you reload, and the hoodie will never arrive. **Online shopping scams** are among the most common scams around, and they work because they look exactly like the real thing until the moment you've paid.

Here's how to spot them, checked in about two minutes.

## The usual online shopping scams

### The fake shop

A full website, often a copy of a real brand's look, selling heavily discounted goods. You pay, you get a confirmation email, and then nothing. Sometimes you get something cheap and completely different, which makes it harder to dispute.

### The too-good social media ad

Ads on social platforms for "warehouse clearance", "closing down sale" or "last stock" on branded goods. The ad links to a fake shop. These are often only live for a few weeks before the shop vanishes and reappears under a new name.

### The marketplace seller who wants to go off-platform

You find something on a marketplace, and the seller suggests paying by bank transfer "to avoid fees", or asks for a deposit to hold the item. The moment you pay outside the marketplace's checkout, its buyer protection stops applying.

### The fake delivery problem

After a real order, you get a text saying the parcel is stuck and needs a small fee. It's not from the courier. We covered this one in [how to tell if a text is a scam](/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-text-is-a-scam).

## Seven checks before you pay

### 1. Read the web address slowly

Fake shops love addresses that are almost right: an extra word, a hyphen, a different ending. If you got there from an ad, type the brand's real address yourself and see if the deal exists there.

### 2. Search the shop name with "scam" and "reviews"

Thirty seconds of searching catches a lot. Look for reviews on independent sites, not only the glowing ones on the shop itself, which are easy to fake.

### 3. Look for a real business behind it

A physical address, a phone number that works, clear returns information, a company name. Vague "contact us" forms and a free email address are not great signs.

### 4. Sanity-check the price

Branded goods at 70% to 90% off, everywhere on the site, all the time? Real clearance exists, but not like that. If the price is the main reason you're excited, that's the reason to check twice.

### 5. Check how you can pay

This is the big one. If the shop only accepts **bank transfer, gift cards or cryptocurrency**, walk away. Those are the payment methods scammers prefer, because the money is very hard to get back.

### 6. Watch out for manufactured urgency

Countdown timers, "only 2 left", "15 people are looking at this right now". Some real shops use these too, which is exactly why scammers copy them. Urgency is designed to stop you running checks 1 to 5.

### 7. Pay the safe way

A credit card or a payment service with buyer protection gives you a way to dispute the charge if nothing arrives. It's the closest thing to an undo button online shopping has.

## If you've already paid a fake shop

1. **Call your card provider or bank** and ask to dispute the payment. Do it today.
2. **Change any password** you created on the site, especially if you reuse it elsewhere.
3. **Keep the evidence**: order confirmation, screenshots of the shop and the ad, emails.
4. **Report it**: [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) in the US, [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/) in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (Police Scotland on 101), and your bank plus the NCCIA in Pakistan. Report the ad to the platform that showed it, too.
5. **Ignore "refund agents"** who contact you offering to get the money back for a fee. That's a second scam.
