First, the important bit: **this is not your fault.** Scams are built by people whose full-time job is getting past exactly the kind of care you normally take. They catch doctors, engineers, bank staff and, yes, people who write blog posts about scams.

Second: if money has just left your account, **stop reading and call your bank now.** Use the number on the back of your card or inside your bank's official app. Come back afterwards. We'll wait.

Still here? Good. Here's exactly **what to do if you've been scammed**, in the order that matters.

## The first hour: stop the bleeding

### Call your bank or payment provider

This is the single most important step, and speed changes the outcome. Tell them:

- what you paid, how, and when
- who you paid (account name, number, website, phone number)
- that it was a scam, in those words

Card payments and some transfers can be stopped, reversed or disputed if they hear quickly. Gift cards, cryptocurrency and cash are much harder, which is precisely why scammers ask for them.

### Lock your accounts

If you shared a password, change it now, and anywhere you've reused it. If you shared a one-time code, tell the company that sent it. Turn on two-step verification for email first, because whoever controls your email can reset everything else.

### Stop talking to them

Don't reply, don't argue, don't explain that you know it was a scam. Block the number or account. If they try to contact you again with "one more step to release your refund", that's the same scam wearing a different hat.

## The first day: report it

Reporting feels pointless when you're upset. It isn't. Reports are how scam accounts and websites get shut down, and in some cases how money gets traced.

| Where you are | Report to |
| --- | --- |
| **UK (England, Wales, Northern Ireland)** | [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/), online or 0300 123 2040 |
| **UK (Scotland)** | Police Scotland on 101 |
| **UK, scam texts** | Forward to 7726 (free) |
| **US** | [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/), plus the FBI's [IC3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/) for online scams |
| **US, identity theft** | [IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov/) |
| **Pakistan** | Your bank or wallet first, then a complaint with the **NCCIA** (National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency) |

Also report the scam on the platform where it happened: the marketplace, social network, dating app or messaging app. They can remove the account before it hits someone else.

Keep everything: screenshots of messages, the website address, payment receipts, phone numbers, usernames. Write down what happened while it's fresh. Every report will ask for the same details, and you'll be glad to have them in one place.

## The first week: protect yourself

**Watch your accounts.** Check bank statements and card activity for anything you don't recognise.

**Expect a second attempt.** Scammers share lists of people who've paid before. Be extra suspicious of anyone who contacts you in the next few weeks, especially about your case.

**Beware the "recovery" offer.** This one deserves its own warning. Shortly after a scam, people often get contacted by someone offering to recover the money for a fee: a "lawyer", a "fund recovery agency", sometimes even someone claiming to be from the police. It's a second scam aimed at people who've just lost money. Real help from your bank, police and official agencies doesn't come with an upfront fee.

**Tell someone.** A friend, family member, or colleague. Partly because it helps to say it out loud. Partly because they may be next, and now they'll recognise it.

## Why scams work on smart people

Scams don't target stupidity. They target situations: being rushed, being scared ("your account has been compromised"), being excited ("you've won"), or being kind ("Mum, I've lost my phone"). In those moments, everyone's judgement slips a little. The scammer's script is designed to keep you in that moment until the money moves.

That's why the best protection isn't "be smarter". It's having a habit for the moment something feels off: stop, check through an official channel, and ask someone.
